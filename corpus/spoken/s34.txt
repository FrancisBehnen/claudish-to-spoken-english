Here is the whole picture in one go, because it took a while to work out and the pieces only make sense together. The plugin watches every message the assistant puts on screen, buffers the streamed pieces to disk, and only calls the model once the last piece has arrived. That is why a rewrite never appears halfway through a sentence. If anything at all goes wrong, the hook prints nothing and exits quietly, so the original text stays where it is. That rule is not negotiable, and every other choice in the design bends around it. The speech side has to obey the same rule, which is why it runs as a detached process with its own timeout rather than as another step in the hook. The worker stays resident because loading the model costs about a second every time, and paying that on each message would be absurd. It settles at roughly six hundred and sixty five megabytes under load, drops to about three hundred and eighty when idle, and falls as low as twenty eight when it is genuinely doing nothing. It survives sleep and wake without leaking, though the first sentence after a wake takes almost five seconds instead of one. None of that was guessed. All of it was measured on this machine, over several hours, with the numbers written down as they came in rather than reconstructed afterwards from memory.
